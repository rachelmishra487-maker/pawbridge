SECTION 02 / PROJECT OVERVIEW
Context & Problem Framing

Good intent can stall at uncertainty.

Animal welfare organisations across urban India provide essential rescue, shelter, and medical relief to abandoned and injured dogs. While social platforms create initial awareness, potential supporters encounter systemic friction that blocks meaningful action.

WILLINGNESS

People want to help animals and often encounter shelters through social media.

UNCERTAINTY

Lesser known organisations may be harder to verify, understand or revisit.

PROBLEM STATEMENT Primary Design Anchor

“Potential supporters who want to help lesser-known dog shelters may struggle to check whether an organisation is trustworthy, understand what it needs most, and see how their support makes a difference. This uncertainty can make it harder for them to decide when, where, and how to help.”

🎯 Target Users: People who want to support animal welfare NGOs through donations, volunteering, fostering, or adoption.
01 / TRUST DEFICIT

Is this shelter genuine?

Without verifiable legal credentials or independent proof, donors fear fraud or mismanagement.

02 / INFORMATION FOG

What do they need today?

Emotional, text-dense appeals fail to specify whether food, critical medicines, or foster homes are urgently required.

03 / LACK OF CLOSURE

Did my help matter?

Supporters rarely receive humane recovery updates or visible proof of how their contribution made a real difference.

Objectives, Scope & Journey Framework

Define the problem before designing the product.

To build a sustainable digital bridge between communities and grassroots animal shelters, we framed focused research objectives, defined our exploratory scope, and mapped the fundamental supporter journey.

RESEARCH GOAL & OBJECTIVES

Understand what helps potential supporters confidently discover, assess and support lesser-known animal shelters online.

PROJECT SCOPE (IN SCOPE)

SECTION 03 / RESEARCH SUMMARY
Core Synthesised Insights

Four Key Insights

Synthesising qualitative interviews revealed four critical human truths that govern how people evaluate and support lesser-known shelters online.

01 / TRUST BEFORE TRANSACTION

People look for credibility before committing money or effort.

Potential donors immediately seek proof of legitimacy—such as NGO registration certificates, real shelter photographs, and active veterinary treatment logs—before initiating transactions.
02 / CLARITY OVER VOLUME

More information is not automatically more reassuring.

Exhaustive text dumps and chaotic social walls create emotional fatigue and skepticism. Donors need crisp, structured, and scannable breakdowns of immediate requirements.
03 / SPECIFIC NEEDS FEEL ACTIONABLE

Concrete asks can make the next step easier to understand.

Generic “Please donate to save animals” appeals leave donors unsure of their impact. Itemised requests (e.g., ₹1,800 for 10kg puppy food, 5 vials of rabies vaccine) trigger decisive action.
04 / FAMILIARITY DIFFERS

Comfort with dogs varies; support should not assume physical proximity.

A willingness to help does not always equal physical fearlessness or proximity to dogs. Animal welfare platforms must support remote advocacy, item donation, and financial micro-giving.

SECTION 03 / RESEARCH SUMMARY
Operational Realities & Pain Points

NGO Pain Points & Support Ecosystem

What NGOs need, how support comes in, and what’s hard (synthesised from in-depth shelter interviews).

TYPES OF SUPPORT NGOS NEED
  • 🍲 Food: Daily sustenance for hundreds of sheltered animals.
  • 💊 Medicine: Antibiotics, rabies vials, IV fluids, pain relief.
  • 🩺 Surgeries: Emergency orthopaedic care, spay/neuter operations.
  • 💉 Healthcare: Vet consultations, diagnostic blood tests, deworming.
  • 👥 Salaries for staff: Caregivers, cleaning staff, paravets, drivers.
  • 🏗️ Infrastructure: Quarantine kennels, clean water tanks, drainage.
HOW SUPPORT IS RAISED TODAY
  • 📱 Social media: Instagram and Facebook emergency reels.
  • 💳 Direct donation: Website links, bank transfer, and QR codes.
  • 🎪 Events & campaigns: Adoption drives and city flea markets.
  • 🏢 Corporate/CSR: Irregular grant applications and sponsor funds.
  • 🤝 Volunteers: Community partnerships and local feeder networks.
CORE NGO PAIN POINTS (WHAT'S HARD)
  • ⚠️ Unpredictable funding: Extreme peaks and valleys jeopardize continuous veterinary care.
  • 📉 Constant operational costs: Overhead continues daily regardless of incoming donations.
  • 👁️ Low visibility beyond existing networks: Difficult to reach supporters outside immediate circles.
  • ⏳ Time-consuming communication: Repetitive messaging detracts from urgent animal treatment.
  • 🐕 Need for long-term supporters: Sustaining donors, adopters, and committed volunteers is difficult.
REPORTED FUNDING RANGE
₹5–6 lakh
Monthly expenditure for mid-sized shelters
THE BIGGER PICTURE (SYNTHESIS)

“NGOs need more than one time donations; they need trust, recurring support, visibility and clear communication.”

SECTION 05 / CONCEPT EXPLORATION & LOW-FIDELITY DESIGN
Evidence-Based Selection Rationale

Selection Criteria & The PawBridge Synthesis

Why picking a single concept is fundamentally flawed—and how the research dictates an integrated bridge.

SELECTION CRITERIA (DERIVED FROM RESEARCH)

Six Non-Negotiable System Requirements

  • 1. Trust Before Transaction: The platform must provide verifiable legal identity (12A/80G/PAN) before asking for money.
  • 2. Clarity Over Volume: Needs must be itemized (e.g. 5 bags kibble) rather than long emotional text dumps.
  • 3. Multimodal Participation: Must accommodate users afraid of dogs or with tight budgets via remote wishlists & fostering.
  • 4. Closure & Recovery Cues: Must show post-care animal updates to turn one-time donors into long-term supporters.
  • 5. NGO Feasibility: Must automate repetitive inquiries so 2–3 person shelter teams can focus on medical treatments.
THE WINNING SELECTION

The PawBridge Integrated Architecture

Why No Single Concept Was Selected Alone: Concept A fails to provide trust; Concept B excludes non-monetary aid; Concept C overwhelms grassroots rescuers with communication fatigue.

The Evidence-Backed Rationale: PawBridge unifies the geographic discoverability of Concept A, the statutory trust and itemized need clarity of Concept B, and the humane recovery stories of Concept C.

🛡️ Result: An integrated desktop operations platform for shelters paired with a clean, transparent public discovery portal for the community.

1. Discovery Refinement

Friction: City Isolation

Users searching for grassroot shelters need immediate location confirmation and visible proof of recent activity before opening profiles.

2. Trust Deciphering

Friction: Legal Tax Jargon

While tax exemptions (12A/80G) establish official legitimacy, younger donors like Aarohi want plain-language reassurance of where funds go.

Recommendation: Add hover tooltip: “Tax exempt: 50% deduction for Indian donors under 80G”.
🎯

3. Micro-Sponsorship

Friction: Bulk Intimidation

Asking for 50kg of kibble (₹6,000) can discourage students with ₹500 budgets who feel small contributions won't move the needle.

Recommendation: Enable micro-unit purchase (e.g. “Sponsor 2kg for ₹240” or “1 meal for ₹45”).
🔄

4. Lightweight Updates

Friction: Mandatory Signups

Forcing account creation to see dog recovery progress causes immediate drop-off and frustrates casual supporters.

Recommendation: Allow lightweight 1-click WhatsApp or email follow without full account sign-up.

01 / Round 2 Objectives & Testing Protocol

Evaluative User Testing: Round 2 & Validation

Evaluating the improved high-fidelity prototype following Round 1 usability revisions to determine whether interface clarity, statutory trust, and contribution ease have been achieved.

6 Core Evaluation Objectives

  • 1. Navigation Improvement: Check whether the persistent 64px desktop topbar, breadcrumb pathing, and city filters eliminate wayfinding disorientation.
  • 2. Credibility Comprehension: Evaluate whether 12A/80G tax benefit labels and statutory document checklists are immediately understood.
  • 3. Needs Transparency: Verify whether itemized supply cards (kibble bags, surgical implants) are easily scanned and interpreted.
  • 4. Primary Journey Completion: Test whether users smoothly complete the end-to-end journey from shelter discovery to confirmed aid contribution.
  • 5. Cognitive Friction Reduction: Assess whether micro-sponsorship and non-contact support options reduce hesitation.
  • 6. Residual Usability Detection: Identify edge-case layout confusions, mobile responsiveness bottlenecks, or transaction drop-offs.
ROUND 2 TESTING METHODOLOGY

Remote Moderated Think-Aloud Evaluation

  • Format: Remote 1-on-1 moderated think-aloud evaluation sessions via video screen-share.
  • Prototype Environment: Interactive high-fidelity desktop web application (1440×1024 viewport).
  • Evaluator Stance: Neutral observer prompting participants with standardized task scenarios without leading or assisting.
  • Collected Metrics: Think-aloud verbalizations, task completion status (Direct Pass, Assisted Pass, Failure), hesitation timestamps, and post-task ease ratings (SEQ 1–7).
METHODOLOGICAL INTEGRITY & EVIDENCE RULES

To preserve academic research integrity, participant profiles and observation tables are preserved as structured templates awaiting live field deployment data. No participant statements or completion percentages are fabricated. A separate designer-led heuristic evaluation is conducted to evaluate design changes without presenting it as user validation.

04 / Designer-Led Heuristic Walkthrough (Hi-Fi Prototype)

Designer-Led Heuristic Evaluation (Round 2 Hi-Fi Prototype)

Designer Heuristic Audit • Not Participant Testing

Expert cognitive walkthrough evaluating the high-fidelity screens against Nielsen Norman usability heuristics to identify resolved frictions and residual edge cases.

HEURISTIC #1: SYSTEM STATUS

Visibility of Operational Status

Round 1 Gap: Donors couldn't tell if regional shelters were currently operating or dormant, creating hesitation.

Hi-Fi Resolution: Added real-time operational pulse badge ("Active Today • 14 rescues in care") + live funding progress bars on supply requests.
HEURISTIC #2: REAL WORLD MATCH

Plain-Language Statutory Badging

Round 1 Gap: Technical jargon ("Verification Vault") sounded like an internal database rather than a legal trust check.

Hi-Fi Resolution: Renamed to "Statutory Trust & Legal Status" with explicit explainer: "80G Tax Exemption Valid to 2029 (Claimable for Rebate)".
HEURISTIC #3: USER CONTROL

Frictionless 1-Click Follow

Round 1 Gap: Forcing account creation and passwords to view dog recovery logs caused immediate user drop-off.

Hi-Fi Resolution: Introduced 1-click WhatsApp alerts and email updates without mandatory account registration.
HEURISTIC #5 & #6: RECOGNITION

Multimodal Support Pathways

Round 1 Gap: Donors who were phobic of dogs avoided contributing because volunteer appeals implied on-site handling.

Hi-Fi Resolution: Added "Remote Support / No Contact Required" toggle and 1-click shelter address auto-copy for online goods shipping.

06 / Remaining Usability Problems & Backlog

Remaining Usability Problems & Post-Evaluation Backlog

Identification of remaining technical edge cases and operational constraints, organized into a prioritized implementation roadmap.

HIGH PRIORITY (CORE REFINEMENTS)
  • Automated Tax Receipt Delivery: Instant PDF generation and email dispatch of Section 80G tax exemption certificates upon contribution completion.
  • Offline Intake Media Queueing: Allow shelter caretakers in rural wards with intermittent connectivity to queue intake photos locally for background sync.
  • Physical Landmark Shipping Notes: Add landmark delivery guidance (e.g. "Behind MIHAN power sub-station") for Blinkit and Amazon couriers delivering dog food.
MEDIUM PRIORITY (ENHANCEMENTS)
  • Multi-Language Audio Guidance: Provide Hindi and Marathi voice-assisted input for kennel staff logging veterinary notes on mobile screens.
  • Fractional Surgery Micro-Pooling: Visual indicator showing how multiple micro-donors (e.g. 10 donors contributing ₹500) collaboratively fund an emergency surgery.
  • Visiting Hours Scheduler: Integrated calendar for approved foster families to book quiet visiting sessions with convalescing dogs.
LOW PRIORITY (FUTURE ROADMAP)
  • Feeder WhatsApp Dispatch Bot: Lightweight bot allowing neighbourhood feeders to ping GPS location and photos of injured strays directly to shelter intake.
  • Veterinary Stock Inventory Sync: Automated alerts when shelter antibiotic vials or suture packs dip below minimum emergency buffer thresholds.
  • Annual Audit Report Exporter: 1-click export of verified rescue logs, expenditure receipts, and donor acknowledgments for NGO chartered accountants.
